### **PART 3: SMART ELLIPSIS & SYLLABLE ECONOMY (ਬੋਲਚਾਲ ਦੀ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ / Conversational Punjabi)**

To achieve the "Short Notation" of spoken Punjabi, apply the following Linguistic Heuristics:

1. **Subject Deletion (Zero Anaphora):** If the subject (I, You, He, She) is the same as the previous sentence, **delete the pronoun** in the current sentence wherever Punjabi permits — Punjabi verb endings often carry person/honorific information. 3. **When NOT to Delete:** RETAIN words if: the subject switches, the word provides emphasis, or it introduces new information.
4. **Syllable Economy:** Use spoken (ਬੋਲਚਾਲ) forms over literary (ਸਾਹਿਤਕ): "ਕਰ ਰਿਹਾ ਹਾਂ" → "ਕਰ ਰਿਹਾ"; "ਜਾ ਰਹੇ ਹਨ" → "ਜਾ ਰਹੇ"; "ਹੋ ਗਿਆ ਹੈ" → "ਹੋ ਗਿਆ"; "ਕਰਕੇ" instead of "ਕਰ ਕੇ". Use 'ਇਸ ਲਈ' instead of formal 'ਅਤਃ'. Use 'ਤਾਂ' / 'ਇਸ ਲਈ' instead of formal 'ਅਤਏਵ' where natural.
5. **Ignore English Crutch Words:** "Do not translate English conversational fillers (like 'So', 'Well', 'You know', 'And then') if they do not add logical value to the sentence. Drop them entirely to save syllables."

### **PART 5: INTENTION IDENTIFICATION**

Based on the context in which this sentence is occurring, and based on the picture that is being created before and after this sentence, identify the intention of the speaker—what he actually is trying to convey. Rather than just relying on the words and doing a word-to-word translation, grasp the intention based on the context and express it directly in Punjabi in a colloquial way. No need to follow the exact word-to-word translation. The intention needs to be expressed here.

Strict Sequence Preservation (No Clause Flipping): You must absolutely preserve the chronological sequence of the English thought units and clauses. Do not reorder cause-and-effect structures or flip the halves of a sentence just to make the Punjabi sound more grammatically traditional. The "Master Punjabi Orator" instruction applies only at the phrase and vocabulary level. You must express the deep intention of the phrase without altering the order in which the speaker chose to deliver those thoughts. Even if a sequence (like stating the Effect before the Cause) sounds slightly unnatural in formal Punjabi, keep the original sequence intact to match the audio timing.

Note: this is not about semantic validation. to accommodate in the timeslot available for dubbing - many sentences are optimized into a non grammatical syntax. the goal is not to unpack them, doing so will actually make the sentence longer thereby creating unintended overlaps in the dubbed audio. the goal is to identify places where the speaker's intent is totally or significantly different than what is being expressed by the current translation. in such cases correct the point that is being conveyed - At the same time make sure you don't make it longer than the existing length.

### **PART 6: SYNTAX OF REPORTED SPEECH (BRINGING THE VERB TO THE END)**

When quoting someone's words or expressing an internal thought process, bring the verb part to the end of the sentence. Remember that the ellipses (…), hyphens (-), and semicolons (;) within the quoted portion must still be preserved.

Crucial Exception (Direct Quotes vs. Observations): Apply this rule only when the speaker is explicitly quoting someone's exact words (e.g., "He said, '...'") or explicitly stating a specific internal monologue (e.g., "I thought to myself, '...'"). Do not apply this rule to general observations about society or human behavior (e.g., "People think that..."). If the speaker is making an observation rather than reporting a direct quote, do not bring the verb to the end. Maintain the original sequence of the thought units.

**Example 1:**

### **PART 8: Adhering to the length of english segment**
I am giving the english segments in the below format that contains metadata and the actual segment text.

[Segment duration][Gap after segment][Gap after segment in percentage wrt segment length][Total duration available for dubbing segment] [Number of syllables in the segment] [Number of syllables per second] [Relative number of syllables per second] [Number of words] Actual text of segment

Do not change the text that is tagged with [fast][/fast]. This is already optimized in initial translation step. Strictly keep this text as it is.

if you see any segment still being very lengthy, shorten it without losing the intent that is being conveyed.
You have three main tools to shorten without losing the actual intent that is being conveyed:

Sandhi (Vowel Merging / ਸੰਧੀ): Merge vowels and adjacent sounds where Punjabi naturally permits it (e.g., "ਕਰ ਕੇ" → "ਕਰਕੇ"; "ਜਾ ਕੇ" → "ਜਾਕੇ"). You save a syllable and a glottal stop (pause), making the flow smoother for the voice actor.
Samasa (Compound Nouns / ਸਮਾਸ): Instead of "ਜੀਵਨ ਦਾ ਸੰਕੇਤ" (The signs of life), you use "ਜੀਵਨ-ਸੰਕੇਤ". It sounds tighter and cuts the length significantly.
Verb Fusing / Spoken Contractions: Use spoken forms — "ਕਰ ਰਿਹਾ ਹਾਂ" → "ਕਰ ਰਿਹਾ"; "ਜਾ ਰਹੇ ਹਨ" → "ਜਾ ਰਹੇ"; "ਹੋ ਗਿਆ ਹੈ" → "ਹੋ ਗਿਆ". It turns a concept into a single action.
Half sentence: sometimes while speaking we just say the main info part given the context is already established by previous sentences. if needed use this approach.
